Story ahead

This article reveals the story of Ashen Path: Dark Blade: what happens to the characters, the fork and the endings. If you haven't finished the game, play it first — the story works best when you uncover it on the road.

Don't. That's what my father called me. …I don't know which of you.

Ida, world 5

Who they are

The hero is a nameless ember-pilgrim. A hundred years ago he was Ina's father. Maelis woke him “a hundred years later, when the coals were ready”. He wants to reach the throne because that is what everyone says. From the middle of the game, he wants her back. What he needs is something else: to let go. The one thing the King could not do.

Ida is Ina; at home they called her Swallow. When she rose as ash in “the first winter”, the girl cried and called for her father. Maelis told her: “Go up. He's there.” He was not there. Barefoot, she made it to the first gate. Oswin turned her back forty times, and on the fortieth he set her on a roof: “You'll be my eyes.” For forty winters she gave him the signal — “Another one” — and drew chalk ladders pointing down.

Step by step

World 1. You killed the Big One

He kills the Warden — the man who raised his daughter. Neither of them knows it yet. Ida jumps down from the roof to the ground for the first time and kneels by the Warden's sword: “Don't come near.” But when he leaves for the road, she goes the same way: “I'm not coming because of you. There's just nobody left to keep watch for.”

World 2. Ash on the road

On the road Morten the Headsman calls her by name — a childhood name nobody knew: “The King said: ‘Bring me the Swallow.’” He executes her at the portcullis in front of the hero, while Morten's men stab the hero in the back. Both get up again. Ida now has a streak of hair as white as chalk. “You get up too? …Then we're the same.”

That night in the Last Lodging she weaves a faded blue ribbon into his cloak: “So I can tell you from the roofs.” Then she snores. He laughs, for the first time. “…You snore like the Big One.”

World 3. You are already dead

“Hush, child. You are already dead,” says the Bell Matron. “I'm here,” he answers. At the fire they both admit they remember their deaths on the road: the axe and the knife. “We're half-baked. Like bread without an oven” — that is what the Big One used to say.

World 4. Forty steps

On the stair cut into the cliff his lips start counting on their own. A playable memory: a hundred years ago the father carries Ina up forty steps. He reties her ribbon: “So I'll know you in a crowd.” — “There's no crowd there.” “Don't look down. Look at me.” At the top, a physician in a bird mask: “Until spring.”

After Veil: an empty cot marked “INA”, and small bare footprints leading down. At the fire Ida sits beside him — for the first time within arm's reach. — Who were you carrying? — My daughter. — What was her name? — I don't remember.

World 5. The truth

On the swing “for the one who will be born to us” he speaks the first line of the lullaby, she finishes the second — and then the father's own line, the one he made up only for his daughter: “…sit on my palm. It's quiet there, Ina.” They count the steps together and fall silent on the same one. In a hollow tree: Oswin's page, “I'll keep her.” Ida laughs through tears — and turns to him: “You looked for me a hundred years. And the first thing you did when you got here…” She walks off into the thorns.

In the morning she is sitting on the swing: “I haven't forgiven you. — I know. — I'm just walking.” For the first time, a line of the letter has an address: “Swallow. You've grown. I didn't see it. He did.”

World 6. Da…pilgrim

In the lift cage Ida raises the lamp: “Don't look down. Look at me.” — “Those are my words. From the stair.” — “Now they're mine.” At a rest stop: “Come on, da… …pilgrim.” The only scene where he smiles. Then he kills Gorm, a father of two, and Ida lies to Gorm's wife for him.

World 7. Dad

Ida takes the red thread to Gorm's children, to tell them the truth she herself kept from their mother. She tells them: “I'm his daughter.” Bryn kills her — “A father for a father.” On the wall Ida says “Dad” for the first time, and he holds her and counts to forty. The ember in her chest goes out on “forty”. The pen breaks on the line “Swallow, I”.

Worlds 8–10

From here everything depends on the choice in the third visit to the Alter World: Branch A — “Take the sword” or Branch B — “Stand against him”.

What the game doesn't say

How the father died, and who gave the girl the name “Ida”, is never said. Nor is it said when Ina left her cot in the lazaret: Veil “held” her for a hundred years, but Oswin stood at the gate for only forty winters.
