Gorm, Greta, Hal and Bryn
Contents
  1. 1 The mines
  2. 2 The Keep
  3. 3 Champion Hall
  4. 4 Through Bryn's eyes

A father for a father.

Bryn

The mines

Greta knits red thread in Miners' Row and waits for her husband to come home for supper. Carved on the children's bunks: “H + B”, and below, in another hand, “and dad” — crossed out and written again. Overman Gorm cut the mountain for forty years so his children would not have to. He took the twins up to the Keep and tied a red thread on each: “So it won't be the pit.”

In the Great Vein, Gorm defends the warmth of the Keep: “Stop the seam and the Keep goes cold. Mine are there.” Dying, he asks: “Tell… Hal… the seam ran out.” Ida: “He has children.” — “Two.”

Greta comes out to meet them: “Well? Did you see mine?” Ida answers before the hero can: “No. We didn't.” Greta winds a thread three times around the hero's wrist: “Take it to the children. Say: from mum.” That night Ida cannot sleep: “So she could knit a little longer… And I lied. For you.”

The Keep

In a casemate under the gallery, Ida unties the thread from his wrist herself: “I lied to their mother. I'll go and tell them the truth. Them. Myself.” He forces the words out: “There and back.” She smiles: “You talk like Big One.” Ida puts down her knife and jumps down to the twins unarmed.

On the wall she sits slumped against a merlon. Ash trickles out from under her cloak. She gave them the thread — “Hal took it. Tied it round his arm. And round hers” — passed on their father's words, and told them who had killed him. “Bryn asked who I was… I said: I'm his daughter.” Above her on the merlon is an unfinished chalk ladder. “Don't look down.” “Sing that one.” “Dad.”

Champion Hall

He takes his revenge. Hal falls first: “He strikes like Father did. No spite in it. That's worse.” Bryn kneels over her brother and does not defend herself. The strike button hangs over the hero — no prompts, the game waits. The hero's hand on the hilt does not shake. For the first time in the game. And that is more frightening than if it did.

Through Bryn's eyes

Then the game makes you play the same night as Bryn. On the gallery a girl drops from the roof: “Not to fight!” — and opens her fist with the red thread in it: “From your mum.” Hal ties the thread on himself and his sister. Ida draws a ladder pointing down: “Turn back. Please.” — “You — his?” — “His. Daughter.” Again the strike button, and the wait. “Go on. I'm not looking.” The chalk draws a broken-off stroke.

“What have we done.” — “What he did.”

At the end, the hall again. Bryn on her knees over Hal: “Get up, Hal. Get up. …Mum's putting out a bowl.” Her dying words: “Mum… the thread…”
