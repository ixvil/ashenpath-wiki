Story ahead

This article reveals the story of Ashen Path: Dark Blade: what happens to the characters, the fork and the endings. If you haven't finished the game, play it first — the story works best when you uncover it on the road.

Branch B — “Stand against him”
Contents
  1. 1 The fight with himself
  2. 2 Dawn on the wall
  3. 3 Worlds 8–10 as Ida

He gave his coal.

In the third visit the hero does not take the sword. He raises his old sword and takes his stance. “Are you serious? You — against yourself?”

The fight with himself

The Double has the hero's own blows, only stronger, and he never tires. “Letting go is for people who don't care.” At a third of his health the Double lowers his sword and spreads his arms: “Well. Strike. You know what it means.” The strike button, no prompts, the game waits. If you wait long enough: “She said: go back. And you came here. So go. All the way.”

The blow. The Double does not step back — he puts his arms around the hero's shoulders, like a father, and whispers: “Forty.” Ida's ember falls onto the anvil, and his own light flows into her palm.

Dawn on the wall

The hero sits by the ballista, his palm on hers. The ash on him does not stir. Maelis sees the palm: “Gave it. …On his own. Nobody gives it on their own.” She blows, as she does over every sword — and Ida opens her eyes.

“No. No. Hey! …I'm the one who always gets up. Not you. You sit. Get up.” — “He gave you his coal, little bird. To you.” — “I didn't ask.” — “Nobody asks.” — “I told you. Not Maelis.”

Ida weaves the blue ribbon back into her hair, puts on his cloak — it is too big for her — and finds in the pockets a token, chalk and the letter. “What — ‘I’?… I'll finish reading it. Myself.”

Worlds 8–10 as Ida

From this frame on you play as Ida: she is faster and rolls farther (see Playing as Ida). Level and gear stay the same.

  • “The cloak's too big. Never mind. I'll grow into it.” Tomas: “Where is he?” — “On the wall. Sitting.”
  • Ida talks Siv out of it with Oswin's words and shows her the coal: “Mine.”
  • On the Reverse Stair there is no one to tell her “don't look down”: “Look at me. …Well, I'm looking.”
  • “Dad. I've finished reading it.”
  • In the palace Morten recognises her: “Swallow. You've grown since we met.” Maelis confesses. At the Judgement the keepers her father killed stand up for her. Her father's soul is released.
  • The forty steps — she counts them herself.
  • Under the King's helm is her father's face: “Don't sit… Go back, Swallow.” — “You talk like Big One. …What — ‘I’? You didn't finish.” The face smiles. There is no answer.
  • The crown: “Not mine.”

The endings are in Endings.
